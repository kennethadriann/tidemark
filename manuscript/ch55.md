## Chapter 55 — Not the Number

He went off after the decider and left me the fire, and I fed it, because feeding a fire is a thing your hands will do while the rest of you is busy being no use. The drop was a deep one from the sound of it, a lot of talk about lines and where the water sat, so I had him gone for a good while, and I sat in the gone and turned the errand over the way you turn a stone you already know both sides of, hoping for a third.

He'd gated it clean. Know your own number before I say a word about mine. It had a fairness to it you couldn't argue with, which is the kind of fairness I trust least, because a thing that fair usually has a second floor under the first one and you don't find it till you've stood on it.

Here's what wouldn't sit right, and I only got at it once he was out of reach of watching me get at it. Maning is a man who reads loads. It's the whole of his trade. He can put a hand on a lashed thing and tell you not just what it weighs but how it'll ride, where it'll lean, whether it'll turn mean on you three lamps up. He does it with junk and he does it with men, and he'd done it with me twice already down here and been right both times. So set that man in front of a friend who's come three weeks to tell him he's dated out, and ask what he actually needs.

He does not need the digit. That was the thing. I could turn it any way I liked and it came out the same. A man weighing whether to quit his built life and go down under the water doesn't need to know if his friend has forty days left or four hundred. The choice is the same choice at either count. My number, the actual figure of it, whole and current and up on its shelf, would not move his hand one way or the other, and Maning is not a man who asks for a thing he can't use. He'll leave a good tool on the shelf if it isn't the tool for the job. He is careful about what he picks up. So he hadn't asked me for a number because he wanted a number.

Then what.

I fed the fire and I didn't like where the feeding was going.

Because if the number isn't the thing, the errand is the thing. Not the answer, the going and getting it. He'd handed me a job that could only be done the one way, up into the count, arm in the air, the file reading me back while I read it, the whole careful three weeks undone in the one motion. He'd handed it to me knowing, or half-knowing, or knowing it low and sure the same as he knows a weight before he's got words to hang on it, that the job and the exposing were the same job. And he was up the deep drop right now not waiting to hear a figure. He was waiting to see if I'd go up and get one.

That's a different thing to be waited on for. A number you can send. A number you could, in a fairer world, have a man carry down to you on a slate. But you can't send someone doing the one hard exposed thing they've spent their whole life not doing. That has to be walked in by the man it belongs to. He'd told me as much himself, a long way back and about himself, and I'd carried the lesson down and here it was turned around and pointed at me: the going can't be sent.

So the gate wasn't a gate on a fact. It was a gate on me. He wanted to know, before he'd put his built life on my say-so, whether the man doing the saying was the kind who'd walk up a stair he was afraid of. And the sour part, the part I fed three sticks to the fire not looking at, was that it was a fair thing to want to know. If I'm asking a stubborn far man to trust me with the last of his days, he's owed a look at whether I'll spend any of my own being brave. You don't follow a man off a cliff on the strength of his advice if he won't step to the edge himself.

I'd have liked it better if it were a number. A number you go get. It has a place, even if the place is bad. This didn't have a place. It had a shape, and the shape was me, and I've never much cared for standing in front of that particular mirror. I do the reading. I turn it on walls and files and other men's loads. Turned around it shows you a man who has spent a life being very good at not being the one who has to move, and calls it sense, and packs a bag to the water's edge and hands the rest back and goes home to the pot. Maning had read that man across a fire in about a day and a half. He'd read him and then, quiet, set him a test he couldn't pass by being clever, which is the only way I've ever passed anything.

I didn't decide anything, sitting there. I want that plain, because a man in a story would have stood up about here with a thing settled in his jaw, and I didn't. Nothing settled. I saw the shape of what was being asked of me and I did not care for it and I stayed exactly where I was, feeding a fire in a place where nobody read me, which was the whole comfort I had left and the very comfort the errand wanted me to spend.

He'd come back off the drop before dark, tired and short and ready to sit. He'd ask, or he wouldn't ask, which with Maning is only a slower way of asking. And I'd have the same nothing to hand him that I had now, dressed up or plain. I fed the fire. The number sat up in the count where it always had, minding itself. It was never the number. I knew that now, and knowing it hadn't bought me a single step toward the stair, and I sat with the two facts side by side and let them be the size they were.

*End of Chapter 55.*
