## Chapter 60 — Up Out of It

The slope that had tipped me down into the deep tipped me back up out of it, and it was a kinder labor going up than it had been going down, which surprised me, because up is supposed to be the mean direction. But down that wet stone I'd fought the weight the whole time, holding it back off my knees and the tops of my feet, setting one careful foot and then the next so as not to run out ahead of myself on ground that wanted me to. Going up I could just spend the legs and let them ache the honest ache, the one that sits in the backs of them and means work and not danger. I climbed and the floor rose to meet me and I was glad of it. A slope you go up asks the legs a plain question and nothing else.

Somewhere up it the floor went flat, and I knew the flat for the place where, coming down, it had quit being flat and started to fall. There'd been no summit to it then and there was none now, only a low crest you'd walk over and never mark unless you were the kind to keep track of such things. Coming down I'd stood on it a bad little moment feeling I'd turned the wrong way, dropping when the whole of the errand had been up. Going up it gave me nothing to feel. It handed me from climbing to more climbing, the deep on the low side of it now, behind me and under me, and the rest of the way ahead going the one direction I'd wanted the whole time.

The cold changed on the near side of the crest. Going down I'd walked into it, the deep water-cold leaking up out of the dark, settling colder the lower I dropped, the walls sweating it, the rock gone soft where the water had worked at it a long while. Now I walked up out of it. The walls quit the deep sweat and went to plain wet, and the soft-worked stone gave over to stone that was only stone, hard under my hand where I steadied on it. The deep was handing me back to the ordinary and taking its time about it, in no more hurry to be rid of me than it had been to have me.

Then the way pinched in and there was water across it, a wide thin sheet wall to wall, shin-deep and no more, lying flat and quiet over the whole floor. I knew it before my foot went in. It was the fork. Coming down it had been the first place the long run stopped being simple, a sheet like this one and past it two mouths in the rock, and I'd read the tended stone into the left mouth and taken it while the right one breathed its cold up at me. Going up I came back to the seam from inside that same left mouth, and the fork was below me now and done with, the two dark mouths of it falling away into the country I was leaving. There was nothing to read and nothing to take. I only had to keep on up out of it.

But I stood in the shin-deep water a breath, because it was an edge and I knew it was an edge. Above this seam the water had never made the rock. It had only wetted it. And standing there I got the last of the smell. The low wide old smell that had been the very air down at the yards, the smell of water standing where it had stood a long time, thinned off me all at once, the way a thing you've breathed so long you quit smelling it comes back to you the one moment it leaves. I stood in it going and then it was gone and the air was just air. I waded the sheet and climbed on, and the deep was behind me for good.

Above the seam the way went back to one, single and marked where I could find the mark. And walking it, with the deep off my back and the country turning ordinary, I caught my head at a thing it hadn't done in weeks. It was working up a line. Getting one ready for the first man I'd meet up ahead, same as I'd readied one coming the other way. A tired man, came up the low way, looking for work, nothing here to stop an eye. Turning the words over to find the flattest set of them, the set that slid off a stranger and didn't catch. I was three or four words in before I heard myself at it, the same as I'd heard the counting on the rope, my head running up ahead to a man who wasn't there on a stretch where there wasn't a soul for the better part of a day.

I let the line go. Not because it was a bad line. It was a fine line and had worked before and would work again, and I'd likely need it or one near it before long. I let it go because the needing of it wasn't yet, and the reaching for it was the old reach, the getting-ready I'd done my whole life in the place where the doing should have gone. Down at the yards I'd not shaped a word for anybody. I'd been the worn nobody at the poor fire and let that be answer enough, and the ease of that had been the best ease I'd had since the kitchen. Now my head was up ahead of me again, drawing a face to practice on before the country had put a face there to meet. I knew that reach. I'd spent a life polishing what I'd say and never once getting to the door. So I put the line down and kept my feet moving, which was the one part of this I couldn't say ahead of time and couldn't hand to anybody, and had to just do.

The run took me back the way it had let me go, thinning and quieting, the marks fewer and older under my feet. There was no light anywhere ahead of me yet, and I didn't go looking for one. There'd be a lamp up there somewhere, the first of the ones that stood over nothing and only said the way was still the way, and past it more of them, drawing closer together the higher I got, until one of them had a pot under it and a face by the pot. All of that was still ahead. For now it was the run and my legs and the deep gone off my back at last, and I kept on up into it.

*End of Chapter 60.*
