## Chapter 58 — The Way Back Up

The wet way going up was the wet way going down with the cold moved somewhere else. Coming down it had started in my feet and worked its way north. Going up it took the fronts of my legs, because climbing wet stone you lift more than you set down, and the water I broke ran off me and back down the way I'd carried it, keeping none of the heat and thanking me for none of it. I found the marked stone by feel where the light quit and kept my weight over it and went up, and for a good stretch that was the whole of the job. A cold you can put your legs into is an honest kind of trouble. It asks you the one thing and you answer it with the next step, and there's no lying in the answer.

I'd come down this same water weeks back with a man's word in my mouth, keep on down then out, take the low wet side, not the dry. Going up I had nothing in my mouth but the name of where I was headed, which is a poorer thing to carry, lighter and colder and no use to the legs. A word from a man who's walked the way ahead of you is a hand on your back. Where I was going nobody had walked ahead of me, or if they had, they'd not come back down to say so.

The wet climbed and then it eased, and I knew the ease before I placed it. It was the split. Coming down I'd stood at the top of it in the dark and made myself take the meaner of the two, the low wet side the far man had marked for me over the played-out dry, and my feet had not liked it. Going up, the two came back together over my head into the one way, the wet I was on and the dry I'd not taken, and there was nothing to choose. The dry mouth sat off to my right, a little higher, a little easier, breathing its old dust. It would have brought me to the same place. I looked at it a moment, the mouth I'd gone past once already, and then I quit looking at it, because up-going there was only up, and both of them were up.

Past the split was the long empty part, the stretch I'd come down alone with not one fire in it, and it was the same going the other way, only now the cold was leaving instead of coming. On the way down the water had gone deeper and older the lower I dropped, the walls sweating a colder wet than honest dripping, a deep cold leaking up out of the dark. Now I was climbing back up out from under it. Step by step the walls went from that deep sweat to plain damp, the air let go of the low wide smell it had carried, and the country handed me back the ordinary cold of high stone. It should have felt like getting somewhere. It mostly felt like the quiet running out.

Somewhere in the easing I caught my hand at my sleeve. It had gone up on its own and taken the cuff and pulled it down snug over the back of my wrist, covering it. There was no one within a day of me to cover it from. I'd not done that since the top stop, weeks gone, because down deep a dark wrist was nothing, dark wrists being the ordinary coin of men who work out past the count, and I'd stopped minding mine the way you stop minding a sound that's gone on so long it's turned into quiet. My hand had started minding it again before the rest of me had. It knew a thing I was climbing slower toward. Up ahead the country filled itself back in, and in a filled country a dark wrist stops being nothing and goes back to being a question, and a man carrying a question wants his sleeve down over it. I made the hand let go of the cuff. Then I left the cuff where the hand had put it, because the hand wasn't wrong, only early.

That was the shape of the thing I was climbing into, and I turned it over a step or two to be sure I had it, and I had it. It wasn't a wall I'd hit. It was arithmetic. The deeper I'd gone the less a number could reach me, until at the yards a wrist was just a wrist and a man was just his back and what it would carry. Every step up now spent a little of that back. The lamps would draw in and then the people, the ways would fork into places with somebody standing in them, and by the time I got where I was going a wrist would mean a thing again and mine would mean the wrong one. And up past all of it the file sat with my number kept inside it, and it wasn't coming down into the cold to meet me partway. It only waited. That was the whole difference between it and the water at my legs. The water I could answer. The file I could only walk up to.

I didn't hurry and I didn't stop. The climb behind me had gone from black to the gray that means the eye's given up on getting more, and ahead the way went on up, marked where I could find the mark and plain enough where I couldn't. I put a foot up the wet stone and then the next one, and the split fell behind me and under me, both its mouths one dark now, and I climbed on into the first of the country that would know me.

*End of Chapter 58.*
