## Chapter 61 — The First of Them

The single way took me up and for a good while it gave me nothing to do but climb it. No fork, no water across the floor, no mark worth stopping on. Only the up of it and my own breath and the marks of a hand coming back to me a little at a time, older cuts in the stone and then less old, a place where somebody had once knocked the wall back to let a load by. Coming down, these had thinned out of the rock the deeper I got, the signs of people wearing away until there was nothing left but the way itself and the water making it. Going up they came to me again in the same order turned around, the country filling back in a hand at a time. I didn't make much of it. It was still empty ground. But the empty had a bottom to it now, up ahead somewhere, and the bottom of it was people.

The tired came up as it does on a long pull with nowhere to put it. Down the run I'd quit doing sums on the distance, there being no numbers to do them with and no light ahead to count myself toward, and I'd learned to walk the pace and let the far end stay the far end. Going up was the same walking. I set one foot ahead of the other and didn't ask the run how much was left in it, because the run up here had never once answered that and I'd stopped putting the question a long way back.

Then, a good while on, there was a light.

It stood up out of the dark ahead low and steady and alone, and I knew it for what it was before I was near it. The first of the far lamps coming up. The deepest one on the long run, the last I'd passed going down when the run was giving up the last of its people and handing me over to the empty. I came up on it slow and it was the same as it had been. A fed flame in a bracket over a bare widening of the way, a cut hollow in the cold stone with a little clean water gathered in it, and no stall beside it, no pot, no back, nobody kept there that I could see. A flame tending itself over nothing.

Coming down I'd stood at this one and worked out what a lamp meant up here, and what it had meant was a lonely thing. A fire kept up faithful with no one by to be glad of it, the way going on being the way for nobody. I'd drunk at its water and gone on and not looked back at it.

Going up it said the other thing, and I liked the other thing less. It said the way was filling in again. This was the first of them, and I knew the run above it. Knew the lamps came on closer together the higher you climbed, one and then another and then two of them near enough to see each from the last, and somewhere up where they got thick there'd be a pot under one and a face by the pot, and past those the crowded places, and past those the country where a lamp had a man kept beside it whose whole work was to see who came up the way. A far lamp burning by itself was the run telling a man how much dark he still had before any of that. Coming up, that much was getting shorter with every one I passed. The lamps were the count laid out ahead of me on the floor, one flame at a time, closing up toward the top, and they'd tot themselves off for me whether I wanted the totting or not.

I drank at the cold water in the hollow the same as I had going down, and it was good water, the one honest thing a far lamp kept besides its flame. I filled what I carried. Then I stood a moment in the little reach of the light, which was warm the way any light is warm whether it means to be or not, and I made myself step out of it before I'd settled into it. Down at the yards I'd sat at a fire in front of a whole town and been nobody worth a look. But that had been the deep, where a dark wrist was nothing and a worn man at a poor fire drew no eye at all. The lit places up ahead were going to start looking at what walked into them. Better to get back the habit of keeping to the edge of a light while the light still had nobody in it to keep from. It cost nothing to lose the habit of the middle out here, where there was no one to lose it in front of.

So I stepped off the bright stone and let the dark have me back, and the lamp went on burning behind me for the next man, up or down, and for no man at all in the meantime. It did its one job the same either way. That was the thing I'd got wrong about it going down, thinking it lonely. It wasn't kept for company. It was kept for the way, and the way ran both directions, and it had been telling me true both times. Down, that the road went on into the empty. Up, that the empty had an end.

The run took me on, thinning behind me and filling ahead, the marks underfoot growing back and growing newer the higher I went. There was no second lamp in sight yet, only the dark where the next one would stand when I'd walked far enough to raise it. I didn't hurry at it. A far lamp doesn't come any sooner for being wanted, and I'd wanted enough things on this climb already that came when they came and not before. I kept my feet moving up the marked stone and let the first of them fall away behind me, and went on up toward the next one, wherever it was keeping.

*End of Chapter 61.*
