## Chapter 45 — The Light Up the Wet Way

It came the next day, or what I'd taught myself to call a day down there, where the only clock was how loud the place got and how quiet it went between.

I'd fed the near-shelf fire through a long dull stretch of nothing, and the held men had sat their held sit, bored, told to stay ready with nobody saying for what. One of them had a habit of turning a bit of metal over and over in his fingers, and I'd watched him do it so long I could have told you the moment his thumb would flip it. That's how slow it was. A man watches another man's thumb because there's nothing else moving worth the watching.

Then there was something moving.

It didn't come as a shout. It came as a change in one man, the leanest of the deciders, the one whose backs I'd been keeping company with. He'd been standing all the way there, taking the shelf in, holding his count. And then he wasn't taking the shelf in anymore. His head had gone up and over, toward the low end of the yards where the wet way came in, the mouth I'd walked in through myself days back, and he was looking at it the way you look at a sound before you've placed it.

I looked too. There wasn't anything yet. Just the dark of the wet mouth and the cold coming off it same as always.

Then word ran. I didn't hear it start, everything down there reaching me a beat after it happened. It went low and quick along the men nearest the mouth and then back through the fires, nobody making a thing of it, just a word passed hand to hand and a lot of heads coming up off a lot of fires. I caught the tail of it off a back going past me, one word said to another man, and the word was "coming." Nothing else that I could get. Just that. Somebody was coming, up the wet way, in.

Then I saw the light. Far down the wet mouth, low, a lamp carried at a man's hip and not held up high, and moving, coming on slow the way a heavy thing comes on over wet ground. One light, then I thought two, then one again as the way bent it out of sight and gave it back. A cold light on the water, coming in.

The lean decider didn't wait to see whose it was. He turned to his held men and got them up with a jerk of his chin and a word I did hear, because he threw it flat and hard across the cold. "Up. On your feet." They came up grumbling out of their long boredom, stiff, too stiff yet to be glad the thing they'd waited on had finally shown, and he pointed them down toward the shelf and the wet mouth both and spread them out the way you set men who are about to take a weight off somebody. The backs he'd kept down through a night and a day and hadn't spent on any ordinary load, he spent them now, all at once, on this.

That told me more than the light did. A decider doesn't stand five backs down for two days on a whim and then throw them at just any drop. He'd been saving them for this one. Whatever was coming up the wet way was the heavy thing the old man had told me about, the far kind surfacing with a load too big for a fresh back, the drop the whole yards had been quietly leaning toward since before I'd moved my fire. It was here, or near enough to here that the men who ran the place believed in it with their hands.

Whose it was, I couldn't say. That was the part I had to sit still inside of. It could have been the man I'd come all this way down for. It could as easily have been some other far salvager the yards was owed, a stranger who'd gone out and stayed out and was coming back heavy, nothing to do with me or the name I hadn't spent on anyone down here. The light didn't tell you a name. A light coming up a wet way is only a man with a lamp, and every far man carries one, and they all look the same at that distance, which is to say they look like a small cold star that's decided to come in out of the dark.

Everything in me wanted to get up and go down toward the mouth with the rest of the leaning, because that's where the answer was, that's where the face would come out of the dark and be a face at last. I didn't. Getting up now, with the whole place tipped one way and the deciders' eyes gone sharp and every back accounted for, was exactly the wrong hour to be a man with a reason. If it was my man, he'd come in through that mouth and up onto the cleared shelf, and I was already sitting closer to the shelf than to the mouth, which was as close as a careful man had any business being. If it wasn't my man, I'd learn that too, from where I sat, and be glad I hadn't spent myself walking down to meet a stranger.

So I stayed at the near-shelf fire and I watched the light come on. It was slow. Heavy things are. The men were down by the mouth now, set and waiting, and the shelf was bare behind them, and the lean decider stood at the near end of it not far from me with his stillness gone hard, counting something only he had the numbers for. The light bent out of sight behind the last turn of the wet way and stayed gone a while, long enough that a man could have wondered whether he'd seen it, and then it came back, nearer, low and cold, and kept coming.

*End of Chapter 45.*
