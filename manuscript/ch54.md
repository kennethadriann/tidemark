## Chapter 54 — The One Shelf

Morning at the yards is not a light in the sky. There's no sky down there to put one in. It's the place waking up to work: a wet cough somewhere, a pot getting scraped, a decider's voice going down a line of backs, the slow grind of loads finding their men. You feel it fill in behind you before you catch any single piece of it. I'd slept a little, the shallow kind you get sitting up with your back to a cold wall, and I came out of it in the same place I'd gone into it, which was the number.

Maning woke all at once and without fuss about it. He took the hat off his face and lay a second looking at nothing, then sat up and worked his knees, one and then the other, and knocked the fire until it remembered it was a fire. He didn't say anything for a while and neither did I. There's a stretch in the early part of a day where two men who both know a hard thing is coming will agree, without a word said on it, to have their water first.

We had it. Then he set the cup down.

"You look like a man who didn't sleep," he said.

"I went at the thing you gave me."

"And."

I could have stalled. I'd made a life out of stalling, one careful dodge and then another. But he'd read me twice already down here and caught me both times, and a third dodge in front of a man like that is just handing him the answer with a ribbon on it. So I told it flat.

"There's no getting it the usual ways," I said. "I looked at all of them in the night."

He waited. He was good at waiting, better than most.

"I can't read it. The wrist's been dark since the water, and there's nothing on dark skin to read. I can't remember it clean either. I put it down somewhere on the walk and it's gone soft, the same thing I told you about down. Not gone. Soft. And I can't count back to it, because I never kept the days up the long run. No dawns to count up there. I threw the tally out past the top stop and didn't feel myself do it."

He turned that over. "So it's gone," he said. Not asking. Setting it down to see how it sat.

"No. That's the bad part. It's not gone." I looked at the fire instead of at him, which was cowardly, and I did it anyway. "It sits whole and current in exactly one place. And that place is the one place I can't go."

"Say it."

"Up in the count. In my file. The score kept my number for me the whole time I was letting it go soft on myself. Never lost track of it once. It's up there right now, dated and clean and tidy as you please, sitting on a shelf, waiting for me to want it."

Maning is not a slow man. I watched him get to the end of it a half-step ahead of my saying it, and I said it anyway, because a thing like that ought to be said out loud by the man it belongs to and not just understood across a fire.

"To reach up and take it down," I said, "I'd have to stand up in the count and wave the arm I've spent three weeks teaching nobody to look at. Reading my own file means letting the file read me back. There's no quiet way in. The number and the being-seen are the same motion. I can't get the one without buying the other, and the other is the whole thing I came down here to stop doing."

He was a while with that. He'd said it himself the night before, easy, boot in his hands, the first thing to go and get. He'd named it like a chore a man does before breakfast. Now he sat with what the chore actually weighed, and I watched him weigh it honest, not liking the weight.

"I sent you to go fetch a thing," he said slowly, "off the one shelf you climbed down to the bottom of the world to get away from."

"That's the size of it."

"Huh." The same small sound. He rubbed a knee. "I thought I was asking you for a fair thing. Two men dated out, know your own before I say a word about mine. Seemed square." He looked at my arm, then at his own, then back at the fire. "I didn't know I was asking you to walk back up and put yourself on the wall to do it."

"You didn't. I only worked it out in the night."

"Well." He didn't say it was fine, because it wasn't, and he wasn't a man who'd hand you a comfort he didn't own. He didn't take the asking back either. He just let it sit there on the stone between us, the price of it now, the way he'd let the two dark wrists sit there the night before, both of us looking at a thing that had gotten bigger since we'd set it down.

I didn't tell him what I'd do about it, because I didn't know. I'd have said I was working on it, but working on a thing is prep, and prep is the hallway I've hidden in my whole life, and there wasn't much hallway left in this. There was a shelf up in the light with my number on it, and a stair up to the light, and me at the bottom of it not moving. That was the whole map. It didn't have a clever way through it that I could find, and finding the clever way through was the one trick I'd always trusted more than my own legs.

A decider came along the fires then, looking for a back that knew a deep drop, and Maning heard him before I did and got his knees under him.

"We're not done with this," he said, not unkind, and went to earn his bowl.

I fed the fire and stayed where nobody read me. The number sat up in the count, dated and waiting, minded better by the thing I was hiding from than by me, and I didn't get up to go and get it.

*End of Chapter 54.*
